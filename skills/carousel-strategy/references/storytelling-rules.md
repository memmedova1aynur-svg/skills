# Storytelling rules

Storytelling is not a text cut into slides. A story has a concrete situation, a goal or
expectation, an obstacle, a decision and its result. The reader should be able to answer:

- What happened? Who or what situation is this about?
- What were we trying to achieve? What got in the way?
- What did we notice, or change? What was the result?
- What can the reader take from it?

## Story types

| | type | what it is | watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Personal experience | something the expert lived and what they concluded | the expert's real event only |
| B | Client or participant story | real starting point, the work done, a confirmed result | privacy: no names or identifying details without consent |
| C | The story of a moment | a small, meaningful moment in a lesson, meeting or training | keep it small; don't inflate it |
| D | The story of a decision | why an approach was chosen ("why I changed the group split") | show the options that were rejected |
| E | Mistake and correction | an attempt that failed, understanding why, the next change | own the mistake plainly |
| F | Process story | the stages from idea to finished result | each stage adds a decision |
| G | Photo or video story | real images carry the event; text adds context and meaning | every frame adds information |
| H | A situation the audience knows | a typical situation, not a real event | label it **"nümunə situasiya"** |

## Flexible flow

- **First slide:** a concrete event, an odd detail, an unexpected result or a familiar tension.
- **Next:** the short context needed to understand it.
- **Then:** the obstacle and the earlier attempt.
- **Turning point:** what we noticed.
- **Step:** what we changed.
- **Result:** what we observed.
- **Last slide:** a thought the reader can use in their own work, an application, or a fitting
  question.

Shorten or extend by the size of the event. Never force it into a fixed slide count.

## Title directions (examples of direction only)

- "Tapşırığı dəyişdim. Problem isə başqa yerdə idi."
- "Qrupda susan şagirdə yeni rol verəndə…"
- "İlk təlimimdə planlaşdırmadığım bir sual gəldi."
- "İştirakçılar başa düşürdü, amma tətbiq etmirdi."

These are directions for a headline, **not events**. Never present them as something the user
or a client lived through.

## Rules

- No invented events, client reviews, dialogues or results. If details are missing, ask only
  for those details.
- No quotation marks around a sentence that wasn't actually said.
- No unnecessary drama or artificial emotion.
- Every slide adds a new event, detail or meaning.
- Don't hold interest only with "you'll find out on the last slide". Don't hide the practical
  answer to the end without a reason.
- Not every story ends in a sale.
- An observation is not proof: write "we noticed", not "this caused", unless it was shown.

## Storytelling vs case study

Storytelling follows **how it was lived**: decisions and the changing view. A case study
analyses **problem → intervention → result**. Combine them when useful: tell the story, then
close with a short case-study summary or a checklist.
